“15小时读书会”是美国亚利桑那大学东亚系和万物艺术研究实验室，四川美术学院艺术人文学院与视觉艺术研究院的师生共同发起的一个阅读项目。

编者按：

一个蘑菇如何激发我们对资本主义全球生产链的思考？我们的性别认知来源于生理事实还是社会行为？权力和战争如何隐形地塑造着我们的感知？在“克苏鲁世”，我们怎样与非人物种共生？面对爱这个古老而深奥的哲学问题，我们如何理解它的本质和它在现代社会中的作用？赛博格如何重塑自然？语言与殖民有什么关系？面对全球环境变化，如何结合人文与科学知识重新思考我们与自然的关系？

“15小时”是美国亚利桑那大学东亚系和四川美术学院视觉艺术研究院的师生共同发起的一个阅读项目。15小时是中国重庆市和美国亚利桑那图森市两地的时差，但这样的时差并不会阻隔我们思想的交融。

我们希望能通过阅读与讨论，一起分享有趣的前沿知识，一起探索哲学、艺术、人类学、性别研究、区域研究等领域的国际视野和方法，一起认识当下，一起建立我们对共同世界的共同拥有感。读一本书是读者和作者灵魂的相遇。我们相信通过这样的阅读，可以获取新知、理解世界、认识自己。

读书会将采取线上形式，每一期将安排一位或多位领读人讲解书本内容。欢迎大家的加入，让我们的思想碰撞出更耀眼的火花。

“15 Hours” is an online reading group initiated by students and faculties from the Department of East Asian Studies at the University of Arizona and the College of Visual Art Research at Sichuan Fine Arts Institute. “15 Hours” refers to the time difference betw een the two cities - Chongqing, China and Tucson, Arizona- where the two universities are located. Nonetheless, such a time difference will not hinder conversations.

We aim to share cutting-edge knowledge, explore transnational perspectives and approaches in an interdisciplinary study that involves philosophy, art, anthropology,gender studies,regional studies,among other fields.

We also attempt to understand the present and establish a community of sharing. Since reading is an encounter between the souls of readers and authors, we believe that through a series of reading practices, we can acquire new knowledge, understand the world, and form a better insight of ourselves.

In each session, we will invite one or two leading readers to share their notes. We are looking forward to your participation that must bring us dazzling sparks.

《游戏中的亚洲历史和遗产》

Asian Histories and Heritages in Video Games

Asian Histories and Heritages in Video Games

Yowei Kang

Routledge,2024

《游戏中的亚洲历史和遗产》探讨了亚洲国家历史在游戏中的呈现。本书超越了游戏中描绘的多元地区历史以及观众对这些历史的认识以展示可用于研究历史性游戏的多种方法——从后殖民主义到身份政治再到遗产研究。

Asian Histories and Heritages in Video Games explores the representations of national Asian histories in digital games. This book looks beyond the diversity of the local histories depicted in games, and the audience reception of these histories, to show a diversity of approaches which can be used in examining historical games– from postcolonialism to identity politics to heritage studies.

Discussant：

董宇翔

迈阿密大学艺术和艺术史系讲师

艺术、教育和社会工作者

他当前的实践和研究由一对核心矛盾所驱动——人类世的民族志与物导向本体论的思辨。他的写作聚焦于艺术、技术和社会的交汇处。曾在《莱昂纳多》、《摄影》、《媒体艺术研究与理论》等期刊发表论文。他曾获2018年国际艺术评论奖二等奖并在泰特利物浦、美国大学艺术协会年会等国际机构和会议发表研究。

Yuxiang Dong

Lecturer at the Department of Art and Art History in the University of Miami

An art, educational, and social worker

His current practices and research are driven by the contradiction between ethnography in the Anthropocene and the speculation of object-oriented ontology. He writes extensively at the nexus of art, technology, and society. His articles have been published in Leonardo, Photographies, Media-N, and other journals. In 2018, he was a recipient of the Joint Second Prize in the International Awards for Art Criticism. He has presented his research at Tate Liverpool, College Art Association Annual Conference, and other international institutions and conferences.

张瑞麒

密苏里大学视觉研究研究学院的助理教授

多媒体艺术家

他将计算作为一种叙事容器，并通过移动影像、视频装置和游戏引擎来探索新兴技术的复杂性。他的作品关注当今人们阅读复杂信息的能力及其对我们日常生活的影响。

Ruiqi Zhang

Assistant Professor of Digital Storytelling at the University of Missouri

Multimedia artist

Ruiqi Zhang (b. Liaoyang, China) is a multimedia artist who works with moving images, video installations, and game engines to explore the complexity of emerging technology and computation as the alternative narrative container. His work highlights the ability of people today to read complex information and its impact on our daily lives while forming new modes of politics, aesthetics, and consciousness. He is currently an Assistant Professor of Digital Storytelling at the University of Missouri.

Ruiqi’s work has been exhibited internationally at venues such as Times Art Museum Chengdu, Imaginary Z Gallery, Hangzhou, Stove Works, Chattanooga, CICA Museum Gimpo-si, The Anderson, Richmond, Towson University, Baltimore, Cardinal Space, Baltimore, and Thatalright Art Space, Taipei, and others.
