前言 PREFACE

游牧艺术项目 Nomadic Art Project

“游牧”一词出现较早，《周礼》中有“囿人掌囿游之兽”，汉代郑玄注为“游牧之兽”，指在苑囿中牧养之兽。《史记》中记载匈奴的生活形态，提到“逐水草迁徙，毋城郭常处、耕田之业，然亦各有分地”。《太平寰宇记》中也提到“胡人衣食之业不着于地，其势易以扰乱边境。食肉饮酪衣以皮毛，非有城郭田宅之归，择肥壤以游牧，随逐水草”。

“游牧”一词在西方源自于希腊语中的“νομ”，泛指从一个地方游荡到另一个地方，以寻找牧场而漫游的民族。从印欧语系单词“nomos”（可翻译为“固定或有界的区域”或“牧场”）中生长出“nomas”，意思是“流浪的牧民部落的成员”，即“有人在寻找放牧的地方”。

游牧艺术项目是由艺术家群体自发的长期艺术项目，旨在通过游走式的艺术在地，结合艺术交流、分享、讨论、放映、展览等板块，计划每年由艺术家轮流组织最少一次的艺术驻地（不同的场域环境和驻地主题），激活艺术家感知和碰撞，抱团取暖在不确定性的当下试图寻找新的“可能性”和“价值”。

The term "nomad" has been around for a long time; in "The Book of Rites," it is mentioned that "the overseer of the enclosure is in charge of the animals kept in the enclosure." In the annotations by Zheng Xuan during the Han Dynasty, it is defined as "the animals of the nomadic tribe," referring to the animals raised in the enclosures. "The Records of the Grand Historian" records the lifestyle of the Xiongnu people, stating that "they move with the water and grass, without permanent cities or settled agricultural practices, but each group has its own designated territory." "The Taiping Yuanwu Ji" also notes that "the livelihoods of the Xiongnu people are not tied to the land, making it easier for them to disrupt the border regions. They subsist on meat and dairy products, and their clothing is made from animal fur and hides. They choose fertile lands for their nomadic activities, moving with the water and grass."

The term "nomad" originates from the Greek word "νομ", which generally refers to a group of people who roam from one place to another in search of grazing grounds.

The word "nomos" (which can be translated as "a fixed or bounded area" or "a pasture") in the Indo-European language family gave rise to "nomas," which means "a member of a wandering pastoral tribe," or "someone looking for a place to graze."

"Nomadic" is a long-term, self-initiated art project by a group of artists, which aims to activate the perception and collision of artists by organizing at least one art residency (in different settings and with different themes) every year, and to explore new "possibilities" and "values" in an uncertain present through art exchanges, sharing, discussions, screenings, and exhibitions.

贵州草海“拾物”艺术驻地计划

Guizhou Caohai “Collection” Art Residency Program

“拾物”一词有捡拾和收集的行为和方法，“拾物”它更指向一种廉价和低廉的方式，此次驻地的环境为单纯的自然人文风景，当地拥有丰富的自然材料，在此次驻地我们将展开围绕艺术分享、艺术讨论、在地创作、在地展览几个内容进行展开交流与互动，我们提倡“贫穷艺术”、“大地艺术”、“生态艺术”的艺术环保理念，就地取材，用最“便宜”的材料做“最深入”的思考和回应。

The term "picking up things" refers to both the act and method of gathering items; it also implies a relatively inexpensive and low-cost approach. The environment at this location consists of pure natural and cultural scenery, and the area is rich in natural materials. During this residency, we will engage in discussions and exchanges around art sharing, art discussions, local creations, and local exhibitions, and we advocate the artistic environmental protection concepts of "poverty art," "land art," and "ecological art," using locally available materials to create "deepest" reflections and responses with the "cheapest" materials possible.

Artist Statement:

My practice spans theatre and visual media, using the body and lens as prisms to intervene in contemporary discourse.In theatre, I deconstruct and reconfigure energies of physicality, sound, and improvisational comedy to create original cross-disciplinary texts. Through a Mayenburg-esque contemporary lens, my works deliver incisive critiques of social realities and individual struggles.In visual art, I employ conceptual and documentary photography to investigate the politics of gaze, bodily complexities, Gen-Z alienation, and fluid femininity—with gender as a persistent core concern.Film photography serves as my medium to interrogate temporal decay and chromatic perception, while also functioning as a tool of resistance:When framing faces, I capture raw authenticity. Beauty becomes irrelevant here—the emotional complexity revealed through visages constitutes irreplaceable testimony.I remain vigilant against the lens‘hegemony: Its nature as a phallic instrument of forced intervention violently freezes time. With every shutter release, it violently tears at the tension-fraught fabric of power triangulated among photographer, subject, and space.
